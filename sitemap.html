Information architecture

Organized around financial work, not features.

The global navigation reflects the recurring jobs of a finance team: understand balances, move money, control cards and people, reconcile expenses, inspect reporting, and define policy.

Primary objectPayment
Primary actorFinance Manager / CFO
Cross-cutting layerAudit trail + permissions

03.02

Navigation rationale

Each top-level section maps to an operational responsibility rather than an internal system object.

Money visibility

Accounts

Current balances, currencies and account-level activity. The starting point for understanding available funds.

Money movement

Payments

Creation, approval, execution and traceability of transfers. This is the hero workflow.

Spend instruments

Cards

Company cards and control boundaries for recurring or delegated spend.

Governance

Team

People, roles and permissions that determine who can initiate, review and administer.

Reconciliation

Expenses

Evidence, receipts, categories and review status for money already spent.

Control layer

Settings

Workspace configuration and approval policies that shape behavior across the product.

03.04

Cross-cutting architecture

Some capabilities should not become standalone destinations because they belong to every financial object.

AUDIT

Activity trail

Every meaningful mutation records actor, timestamp, old state, new state and decision context.

POLICY

Approval logic

Rules are configured in Settings but surfaced inline wherever they affect a payment decision.

ACCESS

Permission model

Roles change available actions while preserving enough context for handoff and accountability.

03.05

IA guardrails

Rules that keep the product legible as more financial capabilities are added.

01

Keep work near its object

Payment approval belongs inside Payments, not inside a generic “Tasks” inbox that strips financial context.

02

Expose policy in context

Users should see why an action is blocked or escalated at the decision point.

03

Do not hide recovery

Failed, rejected and expired states remain discoverable and retain their audit history.
